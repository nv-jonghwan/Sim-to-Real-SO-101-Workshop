DLI Preparation and Delivery#

This page covers installation and equipment setup on the day before the workshop, followed by guidance for classroom delivery. Use the shared repository and skills to prepare each station. Complete assembly and preflight checks so participants can begin calibration and exercises when they arrive.

Today's Schedule · Using the Workspace · Datasets and Models · Troubleshooting

Day-before Setup: Build the Robot Workspace#

Following Build Your Robot Lab in the public course and the current event guide, prepare each station in this order: assemble the workspace, connect the equipment, calibrate, and check teleoperation. Match the lightbox dimensions and colors, lighting, camera views, and rack and vial placement to simulation. A consistent setup makes problems easier to diagnose during class.

Desk Layout: Laptop, Leader, and Follower#

The photo below shows a two-person station from an earlier workshop. Viewed from the participants' side, the laptop sits on the left, the leader is at the front table edge to its right, and the follower is at the open front of the lightbox on the right. Use these relative positions as a reference. Reserve the leader's full motion envelope before placing the laptop and chairs.

1. Prepare the Table and Access Routes#

Place the lightbox on a stable table with its front open toward participants. Reserve room for both manual and motor-driven leader motion. Keep the computer, mouse, and personal belongings outside this area, leave TA access behind participants, and make power connections easy to locate.

2. Assemble the Lightbox#

  1. Prepare five white foam boards measuring 20 × 30 inches (50.8 × 76.2 cm). Cut two to 20 × 20 inches (50.8 × 50.8 cm) for the side walls.

  2. Use the remaining three boards for the floor, back, and ceiling. The finished enclosure is approximately 76 × 51 × 51 cm (width × height × depth), with an open front.

  3. Align the edges and secure the full length of each joint with white duct tape or gaffer tape. For repeated assembly, consider the 3D-printed corners described in the assembly instructions. 3D-printed corners in the assembly guide

  4. Temporarily position the external camera at the side and confirm that its preview includes the robot, rack, and pickup area before cutting a mounting opening. The approximately 5 × 1.5 cm Logitech C920 support dimension is a reference; fit the opening to the actual mount so it stays secure.

3. Secure the Follower and Leader#

Clamp the follower to a sturdy table with its base just inside the front of the lightbox, as shown in the reference photo. Secure the leader in its separate operating area. Before powering on, move each joint slowly to check for interference from clamps, walls, and cables. Leave enough camera-cable slack for motion without allowing it to catch in a joint or gripper.

Important

The SO-101 configuration in this course uses 5 V for the leader and 12 V for the follower. Check the labels on each arm and power supply; do not interchange them. Label the power and USB cables as leader or follower as well.

4. Secure the Light and Set Brightness#

Use a diffuse LED light that illuminates the lightbox evenly. The course setup uses adjustable brightness, CRI > 90, and approximately 4000 K. With a ceiling panel, make small holes and secure the light inside with zip ties. With an open top, mount the light above the workspace facing down. Do not rely solely on tape that may loosen with heat.

If the travel lock appears after powering on, hold the power button to release it. The light used in the course adjusts brightness in 25% steps with its center button. Start within 50–100%, check shadows and reflections, and keep brightness consistent during collection and evaluation. Connect power for the full session, and turn the light off after the day-before checks.

5. Position Both Cameras and Check Their Views#

Mount the wrist camera to see the work area immediately ahead of the gripper. Mount the external camera at the side so it sees the robot, rack, and pickup area together. Identify both USB connections, open the previews, and check wrist focus, occlusion, reflections, and overexposure.

The current event guide matches an external-camera simulation view tilted approximately 54° below horizontal. Follow the camera-tilt procedure: first check the table's level with a phone, then set the tilt. Compare the live camera view with simulation to finish adjusting position and framing.

6. Arrange the Mat, Rack, and Vials#

Looking into the open front of the lightbox, place the yellow rack on the left and the black pickup mat on the right. Flatten the mat and space 1–3 empty 50 mL vials far enough apart for the gripper to approach. Do not stack vials or place them against a wall; orient their caps generally toward the back wall. Leave empty slots in the rack.

This is the baseline layout used to train the current workshop policy. Return the rack and vials to the same areas between trials, and stop the robot before repositioning props. Follow the workspace guide for details.

Prepare the RTX Laptop and Course Runtime#

Before participants arrive, every RTX laptop should pass checks for the container, models and datasets, simulation and camera rendering, and policy inference. The sequence below assumes native Ubuntu 24.04 on x86_64. The Korean infrastructure coordinator prepares the computers alongside Leaders' equipment setup, while instructors and TAs rehearse the full workflow on a representative station.

1. Check the Laptop and GPU#

Item

Preparation and Acceptance Criteria

GPU / Memory

Record the actual RTX laptop GPU model, VRAM, system RAM, and available SSD space. Laptop and desktop GPUs with similar product names have different memory and power constraints. Run simulation and the policy server together on a representative laptop.

OS·Driver

Prepare Ubuntu 24.04 x86_64 and an NVIDIA driver that supports the GPU. The current container's CUDA 13.x runtime requires the R580 driver family or later.

Power / Cooling

Connect AC power and check the manufacturer's performance settings. Keep air intakes and exhausts clear, and review power settings to prevent automatic suspend during exercises.

Display / Input

Provide a mouse and open the container GUI and browser viewer under the actual participant account. Verify that display output and USB cameras work at the same time.

Network / Storage

Check access to GitHub, Git LFS, the container registry, and Hugging Face in advance. Reserve space for the container build, eight model repositories, datasets, caches, and exercise outputs.

USB·Robot

Provide ports and power for both arms and both cameras. If using a USB hub, test camera feeds and teleoperation with all four devices connected simultaneously.

Run these checks in a Host terminal.

uname -m
nvidia-smi --query-gpu=name,driver_version,memory.total --format=csv
free -h
df -h

Assess the laptop against the completion criteria below. Consult NVIDIA CUDA Compatibility for driver compatibility requirements.

Automatically Check This Computer's DLI Requirements#

Open the shared repository in Codex and ask: “Check whether this computer meets the requirements to run the DLI workshop.” The so101-dli-prepare skill checks OS, architecture, GPU, driver, RAM, VRAM, storage, tools, Docker runtime, and course assets without installing software or starting GPU work. It reports met, unmet, and unverified requirements, with next steps. To run the check directly, use the command below from the repository root on the Host.

python3 skills/so101-dli-prepare/scripts/preflight.py --format markdown

Automatic checks are maintained in the DLI requirements. The course baseline is native Ubuntu 24.04, x86_64, an RTX GPU, and driver R580 or later; other OS versions are marked unverified against this baseline. Do not interpret the README's tested GPUs as the only supported models. Minimum RAM, VRAM, and storage capacities have not yet been specified, so report the measured values and leave capacity suitability unverified. Do not add together the VRAM of multiple GPUs.

Meeting the basic runtime requirements means environment preparation can proceed. Missing tools and incomplete assets are reported separately; the presence of files does not prove runtime success. GPU or Docker details that cannot be read due to permissions remain unverified. Workshop readiness requires the live simulation, two cameras, viewer, policy, and physical station checks below. A read-only check does not start robot motion, installation, or downloads.

2. Prepare Host Software and NVIDIA Container Toolkit#

Install Git, Git LFS, Docker Engine, and NVIDIA Container Toolkit. The workshop container supplies CUDA 13, so a separate Host CUDA Toolkit installation is not required for this container workflow. Follow the Ubuntu installation guide for Docker and the official installation guide for NVIDIA Container Toolkit. Verify that the classroom account can run Docker commands.

sudo apt-get update
sudo apt-get install -y git git-lfs curl ca-certificates
git lfs install
docker version
nvidia-ctk --version

After installing NVIDIA Container Toolkit, configure the Docker runtime from a Host terminal. Restarting Docker affects containers already running on that computer, so finish or stop those tasks during the day-before setup first.

sudo nvidia-ctk runtime configure --runtime=docker
sudo systemctl restart docker
docker run --rm --runtime=nvidia --gpus all nvidia/cuda:13.0.0-base-ubuntu24.04 nvidia-smi

Verify that the GPU and driver are visible inside the container. This checks GPU access; it does not replace simulation, camera-rendering, and policy-inference validation.

3. Clone the Repository and Build the Container#

Use the aidayseoul2026 branch of the Seoul workshop fork. From a Host terminal, clone into a new directory, retrieve the Git LFS assets, and build the container image. aidayseoul2026 branch of the Seoul workshop fork

git lfs install
cd ~
git clone --branch aidayseoul2026 --single-branch https://github.com/nv-jonghwan/Sim-to-Real-SO-101-Workshop.git
cd Sim-to-Real-SO-101-Workshop
git lfs pull
git branch --show-current
git rev-parse HEAD
docker build -t so101-workshop -f docker/Dockerfile .
mkdir -p outputs datasets models

Confirm that the branch is aidayseoul2026 and record the day-before source commit so every laptop uses the same revision. These commands assume a new installation. If a directory with the same name already exists, inspect its work before choosing the installation location.

Follow the container startup procedure from the repository root. This course uses Isaac Lab, Newton, ovrtx, LeRobot, and GR00T in the same image. Keep the container terminal available for later exercises; use the documented connection procedure when opening another shell.

5. Set Lesson-Policy Precision#

Check the following two values in the config.json of each of the seven lesson policies. Stop the corresponding policy server and preserve the original settings before making changes.

"model_params_fp32": false,
"use_bf16": true

Select the file for the revision actually used in evaluation. The default Host cache path is ~/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--<owner>--<model>/snapshots/<revision>/config.json. For a local model directory, check that directory's config.json. Leave other settings unchanged, and do not modify the nvidia/GR00T-N1.7-3B base-model config.

Hub cache files may link to shared blobs, so check whether another experiment uses the same file. Create a separate classroom model directory if needed. Recheck the settings after downloading a new revision, and restart the policy server after changes.

6. Prepare Starting-Pose Datasets#

Run the following command from the repository root on the Host. It retrieves only the metadata and joint-state data needed by --start_pose_dataset, excluding video. Exercise code reads the same files through the container's ./datasets mount.

for DATASET_ID in \
  sreetz-nv/so101_newton_dr500_20260923 \
  sreetz-nv/so101_sim_manual_perception_newton_20261008 \
  sreetz-nv/so101_real10_sim75_20261011 \
  sreetz-nv/so101-clean-up-vials-into-rack-50_20260628_131121 \
  liorbenhorin-nv/so101_vials_procedural_left_125ep_20261013 \
  liorbenhorin-nv/so101_vials_procedural_all_125ep_20261013
do
  hf download "$DATASET_ID" --repo-type dataset \
    --include "meta/**" "data/**" \
    --local-dir "./datasets/$DATASET_ID" || break
done

Verify that meta/ and data/ are present for all six datasets. Use the sim-teleoperation dataset for HG-DAgger starting poses. Do not use correction segments as a starting-pose dataset.

Inside the course container, check physics-only execution and then rendering from both cameras. Complete the first-run shader-cache warmup the day before. Stop each command with Ctrl+C before proceeding.

list_envs
zero_agent --task Newton-So101-Teleop-Vials-To-Rack-v0 --cameras none presets=newton --visualizer newton

zero_agent --task Newton-So101-Teleop-Vials-To-Rack-v0 --cameras all presets=newton,ovrtx_renderer

Then check the execution sequence for Sim Evaluation, Teleoperation, and the HG-DAgger exercise. Verify each laptop's basic readiness and rehearse the full course workflow on a representative station.

Operational Points During Class#

Class Start and Session Transitions#

  • During arrival from 08:30–09:00, recheck course access, lighting, both cameras, and clearance around the arms. Teaching starts at 09:00.

  • At each session's start, clarify whether participants should watch the instructor or perform the exercise themselves. Demonstrations follow the same course sequence used by participants.

  • State clearly whether each command runs on the Host or in the Container. Recheck leader/follower and camera selections when transitioning from teleoperation to policy rollouts.

  • TAs support individual stations and share recurring issues with the instructor. Follow Today's Schedule for session presenters.

Policy Servers and VRAM#

  • Check model_params_fp32: false and use_bf16: true in each classroom checkpoint's config.json. Stop the corresponding policy server before changing only the required settings.

  • In one instructor's simulation-inference setup, model_params_fp32: false saved approximately 3 GB of VRAM with no noticeable task-performance difference. Verify actual memory use and behavior on site.

  • Stop the previous policy server after simulation evaluation and before changing models. Starting another server while the old one remains active can exhaust VRAM. Follow the shutdown procedure in Troubleshooting and stop only the servers belonging to the exercise.

Robot / Camera Checks and Evaluation#

  • If a policy performs poorly, check calibration, leader/follower ports, external-camera tilt, and wrist-camera focus first. Also check whether clamps or cables restricted joint motion during calibration.

  • Before a real rollout, verify the starting pose and rack/vial layout. Stop the robot before repositioning vials.

  • Compare models under matching camera, starting-condition, and task settings, and distinguish autonomous success from completion with human assistance. Consult Troubleshooting for errors; involve another TA or instructor when diagnosis is uncertain.

Required Briefing Before HG-DAgger#

  1. The instructor demonstrates first. Explain that the physical leader moves under motor power even when the follower is simulated.

  2. Move computers and personal belongings outside the leader's motion envelope. The leader can also move during startup, alignment, reset, and normal shutdown.

  3. Pause the policy, align the leader, and confirm correction_ready. Support the arm before releasing torque.

  4. Demonstrate saving a correction and returning control to the policy. Explain that normal shutdown includes arm-return motion and is different from an immediate stop.

Follow the HG-DAgger exercise for detailed controls.

Environment setup#

Ask “Set up the environment” in the shared workshop project. The agent reads AGENTS.md and skills/so101-dli-prepare/SKILL.md, checks the existing installation, and follows the preparation guide. The same skill files support installation, course questions, and exercises.

To check this computer first, ask “Check whether this computer meets the requirements to run the DLI workshop.” The preparation skill reports basic Host eligibility, missing tools and assets, and unverified capacity or runtime checks. A read-only report does not certify classroom execution or start installation, downloads, or robot motion.

python3 skills/so101-dli-prepare/scripts/preflight.py --format markdown

python3 skills/so101-dli-prepare/scripts/setup_plan.py

Build and open the course guide#

Run these commands on the Host from the repository root after preparing the locked documentation dependencies and Git LFS media. Documentation does not require the robot Container or GPU.

uv sync --frozen
bash skills/so101-dli-docs/scripts/build.sh
python3 skills/so101-dli-docs/scripts/verify_html.py

The build prepares both languages. When you build and browse on the same Laptop, start a local preview on an available port:

python3 skills/so101-dli-docs/scripts/preview.py start --bind 127.0.0.1 --port 8095
python3 skills/so101-dli-docs/scripts/preview.py status

After the preview starts, open the course guide or DLI preparation and delivery in that Laptop’s browser. The local service uses http://localhost:8095; building alone does not start the server or open the browser. If you choose a different port, update the links accordingly.

localhost refers to the computer running your browser. For access from another PC, replace 127.0.0.1 in the start command with the server’s verified LAN IP and open the URL reported by the preview on that PC.

Use the verified URL reported by the preview. Keep the requested preview running; its PID, command and log are recorded locally. Use preview.py stop to stop that owned preview. This is background execution, not boot autostart.

Software preparation and robot operation are separate checks. Confirm the current operator and station readiness before powered robot exercises; a model cache or connection message does not establish successful motion.
