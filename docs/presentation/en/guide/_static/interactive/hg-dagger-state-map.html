Demonstrations

A demonstration pairs what the robot observes with the human action taken next. Several successful attempts cover a band of familiar states, rather than every situation the robot might encounter.

Unfamiliar states

A small action error changes the next observation. For example, the vial tip may end up between rack slots—a situation that successful demonstrations rarely show.

Human takeover

You choose when the policy needs help. Pause, align the leader with the follower, then take control. The workshop records human actions only during the saved correction.

Human correction

Demonstrate the recovery with the leader arm: for example, lift the vial, move its tip over an open slot, and lower it into place. Save the useful human-controlled segment.

Hand control back

Guide the robot out of the failure, save the useful correction, then let the policy resume. The green segment is human-controlled recovery; it is not an autonomous policy success.

Recovery examples

Corrections use the normal recording process that pairs observations and joint positions. Repeat at different failures, ideally of the same failure mode. This is the data which we'll use in a later training step.
