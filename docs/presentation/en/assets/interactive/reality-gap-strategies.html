Sensing: what the policy sees

Lighting, lens geometry, blur, and camera mounting change the observation. Similar-looking failures can have different causes.

  • Camera models lack real sensor noise, blur, and distortion
  • Depth sensors have idealized measurements without artifacts
  • Simulated lighting differs from real lighting conditions

In this workshop

Compare the rack edge and gripper holes in the real and simulated wrist views before adjusting camera parameters.

Actuation: how commands become motion

Friction, backlash, actuator limits and timing affect how a joint follows its command. They can change with wear.

  • Motor models lack friction, backlash, and thermal effects
  • Joint dynamics are simplified
  • Control loop timing differs between simulation and hardware

In this workshop

A commanded position reached in simulation may be approached more slowly on the physical arm. Compare measured motion before attributing that difference to the policy.

Physics: how objects interact

Contact and friction affect grasping and placement. A visually aligned scene can still respond differently when the gripper touches a vial.

  • Contact dynamics (friction, restitution) are approximations
  • Deformable objects can be challenging to simulate accurately, especially if they change over time
  • Fluid dynamics and granular materials are computationally expensive

In this workshop

Watch whether the vial slips, catches on the rack, or moves after release. These observations tell you what to test next, but they don’t identify the cause on their own.

Modeling: how the scene is built

Link dimensions, joint frames and camera mounts determine the geometry of the simulated setup.

  • CAD models differ from as-built hardware
  • Mass and inertia properties are estimates
  • Joint reference frames and zero positions may differ from the calibrated robot

In this workshop

The same joint angles can produce a different camera view if the joint reference frame or camera mount is wrong.

Teach the policy to handle variation

Broaden the useful examples the policy learns from. Domain randomization varies conditions; co-training adds real examples; HG-DAgger adds corrections at states the policy reaches. Cosmos supplies visual variants that still need review.

In this workshop

Choose new data around an observed failure, such as a grasp angle absent from the demonstrations.

Reduce the measured mismatch

Measure a specific difference and adjust the simulation model. Camera calibration addresses camera geometry; system identification addresses physical response.

In this workshop

Improved alignment or motion tracking is useful evidence, but test the resulting policy on the complete task too.
